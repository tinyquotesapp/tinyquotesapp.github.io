A good quote should have a source

Recognizing a writer's name is not the same as knowing where a sentence came from. A quotation may come from an essay, a poem, a fictional character's conversation or a particular translation. Those differences matter when you want to understand the words rather than just collect them.

Motivation: Tiny Quotes has a built-in editorial library of 1,000 sourced quotations by 52 writers, across centuries and perspectives. It includes nonfiction, poetry and identified fictional passages. The author's wording is retained; synthetic affirmations are not relabeled as famous quotations.

What you can check inside the app

  1. Read a quote and its full author name on the main quote screen.
  2. Tap the work/source label below the author.
  3. Check the named work, passage locator, edition and translator when applicable.
  4. Open the external source link if you want to read more. This last step requires internet; the bundled quotation and source details can be read offline.

Exact wording is only the beginning

Finding words in a book is not enough if they were written by its editor, quoted from someone else or spoken by a fictional character. The library's source notes identify literary context. A fictional line remains a literary passage, not a claim that its novelist personally gave the reader that advice.

A translation is also a particular writer's English rendering. The source information names the translator where applicable, rather than presenting different translations as interchangeable wording.

Short quotes for widgets; space for longer reading

A Lock Screen has a different job from a reading screen. Its quotation must fit together with the author's name. The app keeps separate fit-selected pools for Small and Medium Home Screen widgets and Inline, Rectangular and Circular Lock Screen styles. A longer passage belongs in the app, not squeezed into a tiny slot.

Follow the iPhone widget setup guide to choose a style. Widgets require iOS 17 or later and are free; Pro is not required.

Keep a personal collection

Save the words that mean something to you in Favorites. Your own private custom writing and saved quotations from older editions are preserved, but they are separate from the sourced built-in library. That distinction lets the app keep its editorial promise without rewriting your personal collection.

Read with context

The library is for general inspiration, reflection and reading, not medical or other professional advice. A writer's inclusion does not imply endorsement of the app. Source documentation is not a blanket claim of worldwide rights clearance.

Try choosing one complete thought for today: see who wrote it, find the work behind it, and decide whether it is worth keeping.
